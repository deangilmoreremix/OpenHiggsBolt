one_liner: Opens with an orbiting close-up of a physical card pile on a dark graphite tabletop; the light spreads from the pile to the page board beside it; the stack is dealt like cards, hard-accelerating, flung into the grid; the camera chases the deal, rising and tilting back, and freezes on a runway-style overhead view of the full board
use_when: Showing list pages and card walls where "there's a lot of content / it keeps flowing in"; establishing a first impression of information density
length: 150f (about 5s: 0–36 pile close-up + anticipation, 36–~101 deal, 108–150 full-board hold)
energy: high (a mainstay of the rhythm-climbing section; not used as the opening first shot)

## Intent
First give suspense (what is this pile?), then the answer (it's dozens of items, and they fly into the page and take their places by themselves). The viewer should feel "things keep pouring in", with each card slamming into place with urgency.

## Motion core
- First half (pile-orbit-open, optional): all cards stacked into a physical pile with real height, placed on a dark brushed-graphite tabletop beside the page board; the top light hits only the pile, and the camera orbits the pile in a side-tilted close-up; as it pulls back, the "light" spreads from the pile in a soft-edged circle to illuminate the darkened page board — the page board is always a display board sitting on a dark table, and the dark field around it carries through to the last frame
- **Anticipation beat (borrowed from Disney principle 2)**: before the first card is dealt, give one passage-level anticipation — the whole pile presses down slightly + the top card pulls back opposite to the deal direction, so the viewer's eye is drawn to the pile first and then to the deal. Anticipation is done only once at passage level, **not per card** (per-card anticipation would drag down R2's hard-accelerating beat)
- Second half (deck-deal): 26 cards are dealt from the pile to grid target positions in reading order, the dealing interval shrinking with hard acceleration; each card's flight has a z arc apex, an overshoot settle, and a landing press rebound (settle/press is principle 5's follow-through — a fast stop paired with cushioning; cut it and it reads as a hard freeze)
- The camera chases the deal toward the end of the board (faster and faster), while rising, tilting back and turning sideways, landing on a runway-style overhead view: the whole board stretches diagonally into the distance, all 26 cards in frame, the far end darkened for depth of field; once the board is full it holds still (> 0.5s, with only a very slow 1.5% push-in), and a tagline + mono count line rise at the bottom left (the count ticks with the number of cards landed). During the ending pause, the layers come to rest on different frames — card bodies settle first, shadows converge ~3f later, afterimages dissipate last (drag hierarchy, borrowed from principle 5; freezing all elements on the same frame reads as mechanical)

## Sound
Pin whoosh-big to the orbit pull-back + first batch of deals (pinned at f308 in the template film), and whoosh-fast to the accelerating deal section (f340/356). Three tricks to keep rapid landing sounds from sounding like a machine gun: alternate two samples, step the volume down, and accelerate the intervals following the animation curve — when they get so dense they blur together, let the sound fade into a single swoosh; don't voice each card (S2).

## Known pitfalls
- An abstract flood-style approach didn't converge over several rounds; switching to the physical metaphor of "dealing cards" passed in one round (R2/B3) — for group motion, find a physical metaphor first, then write code
- Card textures must be slices of real page elements (Q1); handmade fake cards look fake at a glance; extras reuse real card slices placed offset
- Everything flying at once reads as an explosion; staggered, increasing acceleration is the soul; if the first-half orbit is cut, the deal's start needs some other explanation of "where the pile came from"
- The anticipation/follow-through techniques are borrowed from Disney's 12 principles (the distilled version built into the pixel2motion skill); the anticipation amplitude has a user precedent (2026-07-09: "OK, now I can see it"; the two smaller amplitudes before that were both imperceptible); the drag hierarchy offset is still the default recommendation — when it conflicts with the R2 precedent (e.g. the user asks for faster and harder), the precedent wins
- The textbook ratio (anticipation = 20–30% of the main action) produces too little pixel displacement in a pulled-back camera position — **compute pixels first, then ratios**; promo-video viewers see the wide shot, not the close-up
- `<Sequence>` rebasing frame numbers + CameraMotionBlur's internal Freeze makes PageCam sample the wrong frame (horizontal misaligned blur gives the game away) — for shots choreographed in absolute frames, pass PageCam a `frame` prop to restore the absolute frame (the lib component already supports this)
