# How AI UGC Actually Works, part 2

> **Time:** about 12 minutes. **You will finish with:** a captioned, 9:16 UGC ad that passed a sound-off and sound-on ship check.

---

## Before you start

Part 1 gave you a script, a voice and a talking-head clip. Now you finish the last three stages: **Video** (b-roll), **Captions** and **Export**. Most viewers watch muted, so captions are standard for the format, and b-roll is what hides short imperfect stretches in the avatar footage. Prices for this work run roughly $10-$55 per ad on freelance marketplaces, and agencies retainer it at $1,500-$3,000 a month, so a clean finish matters.

You will need your part 1 clip, script and voice, plus product shots or a screen recording. Use **Video Studio** and, if your account offers it, a caption option in the editing area (menu names may differ).

## Step by step

### 1. Plan where b-roll goes

Mark the pitch and proof sections of your script (roughly seconds 2-25). That is where you will cut away from the avatar.

**You should see:** a short list of two or three moments, such as "product close-up" and "demo: phone onto mount".

**If not:** if your script has no visual moment, add one (a tap, a slap, a before/after).

### 2. Create the b-roll

1. Make or collect product shots or a screen recording of the product in use.
2. In **Image Studio** or **Video Studio**, generate product shots if you have no real footage.

**You should see:** two or three short clips or stills of the product itself.

**If not:** keep them simple, one action per clip, so they cut cleanly.

### 3. Cut b-roll in over the pitch and proof

Place the b-roll over the pitch and proof while the voice continues. Leave the hook on the avatar's face.

**You should see:** the avatar for the first 2 seconds, then cutaways, then the avatar again for the CTA.

**If not:** if a lip-sync glitch is still visible, cut away earlier to cover it.

### 4. Add captions

Generate auto-captions and burn them in (permanently on the video). Make sure they match the speech timing.

**You should see:** readable captions synced to the voice, ideally with key words emphasised.

**If not:** fix mis-heard product names by hand, and shorten any caption line that wraps awkwardly.

### 5. Export at 9:16

Export at 9:16 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts. Use 1:1 or 16:9 only if the client asks.

**You should see:** a vertical video file that plays full-screen on a phone.

**If not:** if it appears with black bars, re-export with the aspect ratio set to 9:16.

### 6. Ship check with sound off

Watch the finished ad once with the sound off.

**You should see:** the hook is clear from captions alone.

**If not:** if the hook only works with audio, rewrite the first caption line or tighten the opening.

### 7. Ship check with sound on

Watch it again at regular volume. Notice every place you pause to work out what is happening.

**You should see:** no moment of confusion from start to CTA.

**If not:** a pause you feel is one a client will feel. Fix pacing or cut that moment, then re-export.

## Check yourself

* [ ] B-roll covers the pitch and proof, not the hook.
* [ ] Captions are burned in and match the speech.
* [ ] The file is exported at 9:16.
* [ ] The ad passed both the sound-off and sound-on checks.

## Common mistakes

* **Shipping without watching twice.** Sound-off and sound-on checks catch different problems.
* **Covering the hook with b-roll.** The face is what earns the first 2 seconds.
* **Unchecked auto-captions.** Product names are often mis-heard.
* **Wrong aspect ratio.** Short-form platforms expect 9:16.

## Go further

* **How to price it:** gig ads about $10-$55 each; price by deliverable (for example "$150 for 5 ad variants"), not by hour.
* **How to position it:** lead with the business outcome ("more ad variants to test, faster, without a film crew"), not the AI process.
* **Where to find first clients:** DTC e-commerce brands running paid social ads (visible in public ad libraries); a sample ad for their actual product beats a generic pitch.
* **Real numbers:** documented case studies show single brands running AI-UGC-style creative at $600K+/month in ad spend for one product. It shows the format is proven, not that you will match it.
