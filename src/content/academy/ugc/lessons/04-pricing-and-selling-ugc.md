# Pricing & Selling UGC Ads as a Service

> **Time:** about 17 minutes. **You will finish with:** a priced gig listing, a cold outreach message and a draft retainer proposal.

---

## Before you start

Many people who can make good AI UGC ads earn less than they could because they guess prices, undersell or pitch the technology instead of the result. The market already has ranges to anchor to. There are three ways to sell: **gig work** (per ad, $10-$55, fastest start), **project work** (a batch, $150-$300, needs your own outreach) and **retainers** ($1,500-$3,000+ per month, needs trust from earlier work). The path is gig, then project, then retainer, each stage's finished ads being the pitch for the next.

You will need a few finished ads (from lessons 1-3), and the templates `templates/outreach-template.md` and `templates/retainer-proposal-template.md`. This lesson is business writing, so you will use the studios only to make a sample ad if you lack one (**Video Studio**, **AI Influencer Studio**).

## Step by step

### 1. Choose your stage

Count your finished ads and completed client projects: none, 2-3 pieces, or 2-3 projects.

**You should see:** one stage to work on now: gig, project or retainer.

**If not:** if you have no portfolio, start with gigs.

### 2. Set your gig price

Anchor to $10-$55 per ad. Price mid-range ($35) with no portfolio yet, higher once you have 2-3 pieces.

**You should see:** a single number inside the documented range.

**If not:** do not invent a price outside the range; $35 is a safe first listing.

### 3. Write the gig listing

```text
I'll create a UGC-style AI ad for your product — $35. Includes: script, AI creator/voice, captions, 1 revision. Delivered in 9:16, ready to run. Turnaround: 3 days.
```

**You should see:** a listing with deliverables, one stated revision round, format and turnaround.

**If not:** if it does not state the revision count, add "1 revision round included" before you publish.

### 4. Choose your portfolio

Pick your best 3-5 ads. Use real client work if you have it, otherwise high-quality practice ads for a real product.

**You should see:** 3-5 ads that look consistent and finished.

**If not:** make one more sample rather than showing a weak piece.

### 5. Write a cold outreach message

Lead with a finished, product-specific sample. Do not explain your process.

```text
Subject: a UGC-style ad concept for [Brand]

Hi [Name] — noticed [Brand] is running paid social ads for [specific product]. I put together a quick AI-generated UGC-style ad using the same product [attach/link the actual sample ad] — thought it might be a useful new angle to test against your current creative. If it's a fit, I can turn around a full batch of variants (different hooks/angles) for testing. Happy to share more examples if useful.
```

**You should see:** a short message where the sample ad comes first.

**If not:** delete any sentence that starts with "I use AI to".

### 6. Price a project batch

Package a batch as $150-$300 per test batch, with revisions included. In the worked example the progression is $35/ad, then $200/batch, then $2,000/month.

**You should see:** a batch quote with a number of ads and a revision count.

**If not:** quote per batch, not per hour.

### 7. Draft the retainer pitch

After 2-3 completed projects, pitch ongoing production:

```text
Ongoing batch production: 8 ad variants/month across 2 batches, $2,000/month. Includes scripting, production, 2 revision rounds per batch, and a one-page test-plan write-up per batch tying results back to which hook/angle won.
```

**You should see:** scope, monthly ad count, price and revision policy in one paragraph, priced within $1,500-$3,000.

**If not:** justify the price by pointing at your previous delivered projects, not a generic capability claim.

### 8. Write your revision policy

State the number of revision rounds before quoting. One free round is standard, anything beyond is new work.

**You should see:** a one-line policy copied into your listing and proposal.

**If not:** add it now; do not wait for a client to ask.

## Common mistakes

* **Guessing prices.** Anchor to the ranges, and go to the top only with proof.
* **Leading with "I use AI".** It invites doubt about quality.
* **No stated revision rounds.** It leads to unpaid work.
* **Implying you will match $600K+/month case studies.** Cite them only as evidence the format works.

## Go further

* **Cost basis:** API production keeps cost per ad low and predictable; local production only changes the math at retainer-level volume.
* **Where to find clients:** marketplaces for first gigs and portfolio; direct outreach to DTC brands visible in each platform's public ad library for project and retainer work.
* **Honest numbers:** when you publish your own results, publish the actual number and how it was measured (see the cross-track "Show Your Real Numbers" module).
* **Revision situations:** if a client wants something the model cannot do well (a very specific product interaction, a shot-for-shot competitor copy), say so and offer the closest alternative. For vague feedback ("make it pop more"), ask: a faster cut, a different hook or a different CTA?
* **Exercises:** easy, write a gig listing in range; medium, write outreach to a real DTC brand with a sample; hard, draft a retainer proposal with scope, count, price and revision policy.
* **Templates:** `templates/outreach-template.md` and `templates/retainer-proposal-template.md`.
