# Product Shots Without a Photographer, part 2

> **Time:** about 10 minutes. **You will finish with:** a composited product shot with a contact shadow, a directional shadow and matched colours.

---

## Before you start

Pasting a product onto an AI background looks fake because the shadows and light colours do not match. Two fixes make it believable: the **Double Shadow Technique** (a tight contact shadow plus a longer directional shadow) and **ambient lighting sync** (shifting the product's colour curves to the backdrop's tone). You need the layered file from part 1 open in your photo editor.

## Step by step

### 1. Add a shadow layer

Create a new transparent layer between the product and the backdrop. Set the brush to black, opacity 40%, hardness 0%.

**You should see:** an empty layer sitting under the product layer and above the backdrop.

**If not:** if the shadow would cover the product, drag the layer below the product layer.

### 2. Paint the contact shadow

Paint a tight, dark, highly softened shadow right beneath the bottom edge of the product, where it touches the stone.

**You should see:** the product looks grounded, with no gap of light under its base.

**If not:** if the shadow looks like a hard line, lower hardness to 0% and reduce opacity.

### 3. Paint the directional shadow

On the same layer, paint a larger, lighter shadow stretching to the left, matching the angle of the sunlight in the backdrop. Set the layer blend mode to **Multiply**.

**You should see:** a soft shadow falling away from the light, consistent with the backdrop's shadows.

**If not:** if it points toward the light, redraw it on the opposite side of the product.

### 4. Match the colours

Add a colour balance adjustment layer clipped to the product layer. For a warm golden-hour backdrop, add a slight yellow and red highlight shift. For an overcast beach backdrop, add a subtle blue/cyan midtone shift.

**You should see:** the product's highlights share the backdrop's light colour.

**If not:** if the product looks tinted overall, reduce the shift until only the highlights change.

### 5. Export and compare

Export the image and view it next to the raw photo from part 1.

**You should see:** the new image looks like a premium studio shot.

**If not:** recheck shadow direction and product scale, the two most common giveaways.

## Check yourself

* [ ] The contact shadow sits directly under the product base.
* [ ] The directional shadow matches the backdrop light angle and uses Multiply.
* [ ] The colour balance layer is clipped to the product only.
* [ ] My result looks clearly better than the raw photo.

## Common mistakes

* Shadows pointing the wrong way relative to the light.
* Colouring the whole image instead of clipping the adjustment to the product.
* A hard-edged brush for shadows.

## Go further

**Exercise (hard):** paint the two-part shadow under your composited object and apply a colour balance to match the background light source.

**Worked example:** in the aloe gel tube case, a soft contact shadow was painted at the base and colour temperature was warmed by +10 to match golden window sunbeams.
