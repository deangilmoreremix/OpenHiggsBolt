# Assembling a Short Film, part 2

> **Time:** about 14 minutes. **You will finish with:** a finished, graded 24fps export with Foley, music and a unified color look.

---

## Before you start

In part 1 you cut the clips and added narration and ambience. Now you finish the sound hierarchy with **Foley/SFX** and **music**, then fix color drift between generations with a **LUT (Look-Up Table)** applied over the whole timeline, so separate generations blend into a single visual style. You will need your timeline from part 1 and [`templates/sound-design-checklist.md`](templates/sound-design-checklist.md) for levels.

## Step by step

### 1. Add Foley and SFX on A3

Scroll through the video frame by frame. Wherever a physical event happens (a screen flickers, a head turns, boots hit pavement), place a matching effect on A3 and align the audio peak with the visual moment. In the worked example, "Electric spark & CRT monitor flicker hum" lands at 0:08-0:09 as Shot 1.3 cuts in, at -6dB.

**You should see:** each visible event has a sound that lands exactly on it.

**If not:** if it feels late or early, slide the effect a few frames and listen again.

### 2. Add the music score on A4

Place your background music on A4 and set it low, around **-18dB to -24dB** (the example used -20dB with a slow, tense ambient synthesizer pad).

**You should see:** music present but never covering the narration.

**If not:** if you cannot hear the voice clearly, lower the music more.

### 3. Enable audio ducking

Turn on **Audio Ducking** so the music drops by an additional **6dB** whenever the voice track A1 is active (the example ducks to -26dB during John's line).

**You should see:** music dipping under each voice line and returning afterward.

**If not:** if your editor has no ducking, draw volume keyframes on A4 by hand around each line.

### 4. Apply a unified color grade

Select all clips on V1, add an adjustment layer over the whole timeline and apply a film emulation LUT (for example a Kodak 2383 style). The worked example used a "Teal & Orange" LUT at 70% opacity.

**You should see:** highlights and shadows that match across all clips.

**If not:** if the look is too strong, lower the LUT opacity; if clips still differ, tweak them individually before the LUT.

### 5. Export at 24fps

Export the finished project at **24fps**, then watch it once from start to end with headphones.

**You should see:** a single video file with picture and all four audio layers.

**If not:** if the export plays at the wrong speed, check that the timeline and export settings are both 24fps.

## Check yourself

* [ ] Foley lands exactly on the visual events.
* [ ] Music sits around -18dB to -24dB and ducks 6dB under the voice.
* [ ] A LUT unifies the color across clips.
* [ ] I exported at 24fps and watched the whole film.

## Common mistakes

* Music loud enough to fight the narration.
* Placing sound effects slightly off the visual impact.
* Grading each clip differently instead of applying one shared look.
* Not watching the export in full before delivering.

## Go further

**Result of the worked example:** the screen-flicker sound hides the hard visual cut to the static screen, and the continuous spaceship hum holds the atmosphere together across cuts. Levels used: A1 -3dB, A2 -18dB, A3 -6dB, A4 -20dB ducking to -26dB.

**Exercise.** Hard: color-match 3 clips generated by different video models (different lighting) with color match wheels or a shared LUT so they look filmed in the same room on the same camera.
