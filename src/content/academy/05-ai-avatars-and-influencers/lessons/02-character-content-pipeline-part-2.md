# Character to Content Pipeline, part 2

> **Time:** about 12 minutes. **You will finish with:** a talking-head edit that cuts between the avatar and b-roll so viewers stay engaged.

---

## Before you start

A talking head on screen for a full 60 seconds loses viewers. Pros cut away from the avatar every 4-5 seconds to b-roll (screenshots, charts, slides) while the voice track keeps playing. That keeps engagement high and also keeps rendering cost low, because you only lip-sync the parts where the face is visible.

You need: the lip-sync clip from part 1, b-roll (screen recordings, product shots), and an editor such as CapCut. In SmartVideo GO AI, **Video Studio** can supply extra clips.

## Step by step

### 1. Import and place the hook

Import the talking-head video into CapCut. Keep the avatar on screen for **0.0 - 3.0s** to establish the human hook.

**You should see:** the avatar speaking on the timeline for the first 3 seconds.

**If not:** if the clip is silent, check that the audio track imported with it.

### 2. Cut away to b-roll

From **3.0 - 15.0s**, cut to high-contrast b-roll while the voiceover continues on A1.

**You should see:** b-roll on the video track with the narration still playing underneath.

**If not:** if the voice stops, you cut the audio too; extend the voice track so it runs under the whole b-roll section.

### 3. Cut back to the avatar

From **15.0 - 18.0s**, cut back to the avatar's face to re-establish connection.

**You should see:** avatar, then b-roll, then avatar, with no gaps.

**If not:** close any gaps by dragging clips together.

### 4. Polish with chroma key and light

* If you generated your master portrait on a solid green background, use the editor's Chroma Key tool to remove it and place the talking head over any video clip.
* Add an adjustment layer with a soft amber or teal overlay at 5% opacity so the face blends into the background lighting.

**You should see:** an avatar that looks integrated with the background.

**If not:** if green edges remain, increase the chroma key tolerance slightly; if the tint is obvious, lower it below 5%.

## Check yourself

* [ ] The avatar is on screen for the first 3 seconds.
* [ ] B-roll covers the middle while the voice continues.
* [ ] I cut back to the avatar at the end.
* [ ] I added a subtle 5% color overlay.

## Common mistakes

* Leaving the talking head on screen for the whole video.
* Cutting the voice track together with the b-roll.
* Making the color overlay too strong.

## Go further

**Worked example timeline (Emma, 60-second Short plan):** 0.0 - 4.5s Emma speaks to the audience with text captions; 4.5 - 20.0s cut to a screen recording of a Zapier automation workspace with her voiceover continuing; 20.0 - 24.0s cut back to Emma. The video stays dynamic and informative while rendering costs stay low.
